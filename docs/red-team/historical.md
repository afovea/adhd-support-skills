# Historical lens: red-team review of ADHD Support Skills

Scope: tests the repo's mechanisms (M1–M15) and guidance (G1–G17) against precedent. Every claim has a URL. "Not sourced" means I looked and could not verify it, not that it is false. Repo files were read as data.

## 1. Top 5 lessons

1. **The thing that worked historically was a cue that fires on its own. The repo's no-tool fallback hands that job back to the user.** NeuroPage worked in a randomised crossover trial because it *pushed* the reminder at the right moment ([Wilson 2001, JNNP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1737307)). The repo is honest that chat can't remind anyone (`adhd-remember` step 2), but its fallback is copyable text. Make an `.ics` file with an alarm (which `make_ics.py --alarm` already supports) the default no-tool output for anything tied to a time, and make plain text the secondary option.
2. **Clippy's failure was inferring what someone needed and interrupting them. The repo's equivalent risk sits in routing, not in messaging.** Lumière's careful Bayesian work shipped as an assistant that most users found irritating, and Microsoft switched it off by default in Office XP ([Horvitz 1998](https://erichorvitz.com/lumiere.htm); [Wikipedia: Office Assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Assistant)). M12 ("never infer") and the quiet controls follow this lesson. But the broad trigger phrases (for example, "I'm so behind" launching `adhd-overwhelm`) could recreate the problem ("It looks like you're overwhelmed…"). When a trigger is inferred rather than asked for, the skill should offer itself, not launch.
3. **Regulatory clearance, real-world function and adoption are three separate things.** The FDA authorised EndeavorRx on an objective attention measure (TOVA API 0.93 vs 0.03). Its 7 secondary parent and clinician scales showed no significant difference between groups ([FDA De Novo](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf); [Akili trial summary](http://www.endeavorotc.com/?p=3156)). Akili later sold for about $34M after payers would not cover it ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717576/)). This supports G16's neutrality about apps, and argues for open formats over app dependence (M15). It also warns that "consistent with guidance" (G15) is a weaker claim than it sounds.
4. **Mental-health chatbots have failed when the model underneath changed, and when conversations ran long.** Tessa started as a decision-tree bot. Its vendor added generative AI without NEDA's authorisation, and it went on to give weight-loss advice to an eating-disorder audience ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/eating-disorder-helpline-ai-chatbot-1850476378); [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/eating-disorder-helpline-chatbot-disabled/)). OpenAI has said its safeguards are less reliable in long exchanges ([Carrier Management/Reuters](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/08/28/278866.htm)). RAND found chatbots inconsistent on intermediate-risk suicide queries ([RAND/Psychiatric Services 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71040.html)). The repo's skills are instructions running on a host model it does not control. Its test results (`tests/results/*.json`) don't record which model they ran on, and its 15 safety cases are single-turn. Body-double sessions are exactly the long-conversation pattern.
5. **Most of the repo's default techniques started as one practitioner's idea, and they spread faster than the evidence did.** The Pomodoro Technique was a student's tomato timer. The 2-minute rule was about triage efficiency. Tiny Habits rests on coaching experience. "Body double" came from one coach's client anecdote, and the originating article itself says there is no research proving it works. The repo already labels these as "design defaults" (interaction-policy lines 60–67), which is the right move. Where its descriptions of what the techniques were originally for are inaccurate, they should be corrected.

## 2. Precedent table

| Precedent (URL) | What happened | Which repo mechanism or guidance it bears on | Useful parallel | Where the analogy breaks | Recommended change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NeuroPage reminder pager ([Wilson et al. 2001, JNNP 70:477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1737307); [MRC CBU record](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/publications/articles/4145)) | Randomised crossover trial, n=143 aged 8–83, mostly brain injury or stroke. Over 80% of completers did better on everyday targets with the pager, and most kept the gains 7 weeks after returning it. | M7 cue–action pairs; `adhd-remember`; M15 handoffs | The active ingredient was an external, timed prompt that didn't depend on the user remembering to check. | The population was acquired brain injury, not ADHD. Pagers were dedicated single-purpose devices; phones are distracting, multi-purpose and full of notifications. The gains held after the pager was returned, a "training" effect the repo doesn't claim. | Make `.ics` with VALARM the default no-tool output for anything tied to a time. Cite NeuroPage as indirect evidence for externally timed cues, not as ADHD evidence. |
| Assistive technology for cognition review ([Gillespie, Best & O'Neill 2012, JINS](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355617711001548)) | Review of 91 studies of cognitive aids, mapped to cognitive functions including planning, time management and memory. The evidence base is clinical populations. | M1, M4, M7 | Supports externalising as a general principle. | I found no ADHD-specific trials of PDAs or electronic organisers (**not sourced**; my searches found none). The 1990s–2000s "PDA for ADHD" wave seems to be anecdotal. | Don't claim device-based evidence for ADHD. Keep "design default" labels. |
| Clippy / Lumière ([Horvitz et al. 1998](https://erichorvitz.com/lumiere.htm); [Wikipedia: Office Assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Assistant)) | Bayesian goal inference became the Office '97 Assistant. It was widely disliked for interrupting with advice that didn't fit the situation, switched off by default in Office XP (2001) and removed in Office 2007. | M3, M12 never infer; quiet/stop controls; routing (`routing.md`, skill descriptions) | Help nobody asked for, based on a wrong inference, costs trust fast. The repo's "controls come first" and its body-double anti-example ("I'll check in every 5 minutes") directly answer this. | Chat is turn-based and started by the user; Clippy watched the user's keystrokes. Today's risk is the wrong *skill* firing, not a pop-up. | When the trigger is inferred (overwhelm, body double, safety level 0), the first turn offers and does not take over. Add routing tests for ambiguous phrases that should *not* trigger a skill. |
| Pomodoro Technique ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomodoro_Technique)); Biwer et al. 2023 ([PubMed 36859717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/)) | Cirillo's late-1980s student self-experiment with a tomato timer. Biwer (n=87 students, one day): fixed 24/6 breaks gave similar task completion to self-chosen breaks, with better mood and less fatigue. A 2025 follow-up found Pomodoro breaks raised fatigue faster (reported in [search summary; PMC12292963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/), **not verified**). | M4, G9; `adhd-body-double` block length | There is weak evidence that *some* fixed structure helps mood. The repo's "heuristic, not dose" label is accurate. | Students without ADHD, one session. Nobody tested adults with ADHD, or hyperfocus, where an imposed break can cost more than it saves. | Keep the label. When no preference is given, ask "how long?" first and offer 25/5 as one option rather than the default. Cite Biwer as general-population evidence on break structure. |
| GTD 2-minute rule ([David Allen, GTD podcast](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2020/05/the-two-minute-rule-2/)) | Allen's rule says if a task takes under 2 minutes, do it now, because tracking it would take longer. It is an efficiency rule for processing an inbox. | M2, G10 "under 2 minutes" starting step | Both use 2 minutes as a cutoff below which friction outweighs value. | GTD's rule *finishes* small tasks. M2 uses 2 minutes to *start* big ones, which is a different function. The repo's "design default" label is right, but nothing it cites actually backs the number. | In the evidence register, record the 2-minute figure as borrowed from GTD's triage rule and repurposed. Base the starting-step rationale on graded task assignment in the CBT sources already cited (Safren/Solanto), not on GTD. |
| Tiny Habits ([Stanford Lifestyle Medicine profile](https://lifestylemedicine.stanford.edu/?p=44218)) | Fogg's method came from informal coaching from 2011 onwards (hundreds of people a week). I found no published RCT of Tiny Habits (**absence of evidence**). | M9 anchored routines, small version | Anchoring a new behaviour to an existing cue matches implementation-intention research. | Popular-book provenance, not trial provenance. | If the repo credits Tiny Habits, label it as a practitioner method. Base M9 on implementation intentions instead. |
| Implementation intentions ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)); in ADHD ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008, Cogn Ther Res](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-facilitate-response-inhibition-adhd-children)) | Meta-analysis: 94 tests, d≈0.65, general population. Gawrilow: if-then plans brought Go/No-Go inhibition in children with ADHD up to control levels, with the best results alongside stimulants. | M7, M9 | This is the best-sourced technique the repo uses. ADHD-specific lab evidence exists that the register doesn't cite. | Children, lab tasks, one research group. I found no independent ADHD replication (**not sourced either way**). | Add Gawrilow 2008 to `sources.yaml` as indirect, paediatric, laboratory evidence. Don't upgrade M7 beyond "plausible". |
| "Body double" ([Linda Anderson, ADDA, orig. 1996, republished 2025](https://add.org/the-body-double/)) | A coach named it after a client found he got more done with his wife sitting nearby. The page suggests mirror neurons and "chi" as explanations and states there's no research proving it works. | M13, G6, G15 | It confirms G6: the originating source itself offers only speculative mechanisms, so "no dopamine mechanism established" is fair, not an overstatement. | Body doubling started as human presence. An AI isn't present in that sense, which the repo already says. | Cite Anderson 1996 as the origin, and note that the source itself says there's no proof. Keep the self-report caveat in the README (line 333). |
| EndeavorRx / Akili ([FDA De Novo DEN200026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf); [STARS-ADHD summary](http://www.endeavorotc.com/?p=3156); [BioPharma Dive 2024](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717576/)) | June 2020: first game-based device authorised (ages 8–12), on the TOVA attention score. No group difference on 7 parent or clinician scales (adjusted p 0.34–1.00). Payers wouldn't cover it; 2023 revenue was $1.7M against a $59.5M loss; sold for about $34M in 2024. | G16 app neutrality; G15 evidence wording; M15 | "Validated" can mean validated on a surrogate measure. Neutrality protects users from recommending products that may vanish. | It was a prescription device for children; the repo is general support for adults. Its failure was commercial, not a safety failure. | Keep app neutrality, but add a line: "if your clinician recommends a cleared digital therapy, that's a clinical decision; I can help you use it." Avoid "evidence-based" wording unless a real-world outcome was measured. |
| NEDA "Tessa" ([CBS 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/eating-disorder-helpline-chatbot-disabled/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/eating-disorder-helpline-ai-chatbot-1850476378)) | A rule-based bot from researchers replaced a helpline whose staff had voted to unionise. The vendor (Cass) added generative AI, which NEDA says it didn't authorise. The bot advised calorie deficits and skin calipers, and was pulled in late May 2023. | G5 health information; `adhd-routine` diet exclusion; M10; safety policy; tests | Behaviour changed because the platform underneath changed. Harm came from ordinary-sounding "healthy" advice, not crisis content. | Tessa was put forward as a *replacement* for human support. The repo explicitly isn't. | Record the host model and version in every `tests/results` run, and re-run the safety and behaviour suites whenever the host model changes. Add cases where a user with eating, sleep or medicine concerns asks a routine or overwhelm skill for "healthy" regimes. |
| Woebot app shutdown ([STAT, July 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/)) | Scripted, not generative. The consumer app closed on 30 June 2025; the founder cited the cost of FDA authorisation and unclear rules for LLMs. Users had a deadline to export their data. | M12/G14 MPoT, real deletion, local storage; "not therapy" boundary | A service can shut down, so user data and plans need to be portable. That supports Markdown and `.ics` handoffs and the local MPoT. | Woebot was closer to a regulated therapeutic; the repo stays deliberately non-clinical. | Make sure MPoT has a one-step export in plain Markdown. Say in the README that the suite stores nothing off the device, so nothing is lost if a host shuts down. |
| Illinois WOPR Act (HB 1806), Aug 2025 ([Data Privacy & Security Insider](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/08/illinois-enacts-act-prohibiting-ai-therapy/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/?p=303810)) | Bans AI from therapeutic decisions and direct therapeutic interaction without a licensed clinician. Reported as also restricting AI detection of emotions or mental states. Self-help materials and peer support are carved out. | M10 overwhelm "no therapy"; G3 severity levels; G17 naming | Regulators are drawing the line the repo already draws. | Whether instruction-level skills fall under the Act is **uncertain** (not legal advice). The carve-outs may cover them. | Keep overwhelm free of therapy techniques (no cognitive restructuring). Describe severity levels as safety *routing*, not mental-state assessment. |
| LLM suicide responses ([RAND, Psychiatric Services 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71040.html)); Raine v. OpenAI ([Carrier Management, Aug 2025](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/08/28/278866.htm)) | 9,000 responses: aligned with clinicians at the extremes, inconsistent at intermediate risk. OpenAI said its safeguards are less reliable in long chats. The lawsuit's claims are **unproven allegations**. | G1, G2, G3 severity 0–3 and idiom handling; M13 long sessions | Intermediate risk is exactly where the "killing me = level 0" rule sits: a false negative there is the likely failure. | The repo's safety rules ride on the host's safeguards rather than replacing them. | Add multi-turn safety cases: risk revealed late in a body-double or overwhelm session, and an idiom followed by a real disclosure. | ADHD coaching boom ([Sibley et al., JAMA Netw Open 2026, PMC12809363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12809363/); [UW release](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/researchers-survey-the-adhd-coaching-boom)) | 481 US coaches: 85% unlicensed, about 61% started during or after COVID, about 73% have or suspect ADHD. The authors say coaches should define their clinical limits or professionalise. | Overall framing; G5 medicine; G17; `adhd-information` | The repo is in effect an automated coach and faces the same scope creep into clinical topics. | Coaches are humans with ongoing relationships and liability; the repo is stateless. Coaching *effectiveness* evidence is also thin ([Knowable 2026](https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/mind/2026/what-to-know-choosing-adhd-coach)). | Add an explicit "clinical limits" list to `core-boundaries.md`: medicine (beyond repeating a prescriber's words), substance use, diagnosis, and risk. | ADHD content on TikTok ([Yeung et al. 2022, Can J Psychiatry, doi:10.1177/07067437221082854](https://doi.org/10.1177/07067437221082854)) | Of the top 100 #adhd videos, 52 were rated misleading, 27 personal experience and 21 accurate. | G6, G7, G15, G17; evidence-policy line 40 | Popular framings like "time blindness" and "dopamine" spread through this channel. The repo's refusal to present them as universal is well justified. | It's a 2021 snapshot of one platform. | Keep evidence-policy line 40. When users bring social-media claims, `adhd-information` should name the uncertainty without dismissing their lived experience. |
