# Academic lens: what the peer-reviewed literature supports (M1–M15, G1–G17)

Reviewer: academic lens, red-team. Date: 2026-10-19.
Method: WebSearch, plus abstracts pulled from Europe PMC, Crossref and publisher or repository pages. **No full texts were read.** Every row below is based on the abstract only, unless marked "secondary", meaning only a summary or a repository record was seen. Where I found nothing, I say so. I invented no numbers. Figures I could not check against an abstract are marked *unverified*.
Population codes: A = adults, C = children or adolescents, GP = general (non-ADHD) population, ADHD = sample with diagnosed or screened ADHD.

## 1. Top 6 findings (most decision-relevant first)

1. **G1 (asking about suicide) is well supported, but it has no source and is worded too absolutely.** Two meta-analyses and an RCT find no average increase in suicidal ideation or behaviour from being asked:
   - Blades et al. 2018: 18 studies, g = −0.13 for ideation, OR = 0.714 for behaviour.
   - Polihronis et al. 2022: 17 studies, 8 meta-analysed, including 8 RCTs; no significant effect on suicide-related behaviour, self-harm or distress.
   - Gould et al. 2005 (JAMA): RCT of 2,342 adolescents.

   The caveats:
   - The evidence comes from researchers, clinicians and surveys. None of it is from AI chatbots.
   - The outcomes are mostly ideation, not deaths.
   - A minority of people report feeling worse. In one EMA study of 82 people with suicidal ideation, 22% retrospectively reported mood worsening (Kivelä 2023).
   - The second half of the sentence, "guessing wrong can", has no source anywhere.

   **Action:** add these sources to the register. Reword to "Research suggests asking directly does not increase suicidal thoughts on average." Either source "guessing wrong can" or drop it.
2. **G7 / CLM-RS-TIME-PERCEPTION understates the evidence.** The register relies only on Mette 2023, a narrative review of a few adult studies that it calls "mixed". Two newer meta-analyses find robust group-level timing deficits:
   - Metcalfe, McFeaters & Voyer 2024: 824 effect sizes across the lifespan, mean g = 0.688, moderated by age and working memory.
   - Marx, Cortese et al. 2022 (JAACAP): 55 studies, "a broad range of timing deficits".

   "Not universal" is still fair: these are group averages, task-dependent and partly motivational. In 38 adults with ADHD, reward normalised time-reproduction accuracy (Marx et al. 2013). **Action:** replace "mixed/few studies" with "moderate average differences on lab timing tasks, which vary by person, task and motivation". Keep the ban on calling "time blindness" a universal deficit or a diagnosis.
3. **G11 ("easy isn't important; effort only as tie-breaker") is contested, not settled.**
   - **For the rule:** KC, Staats, Kouchaki & Gino 2020 (Management Science). Emergency doctors who favoured easy tasks handled more cases per shift but had worse risk-adjusted throughput. Lab work attributed the preference to fatigue and a sense of progress.
   - **Against the rule:** Gal & McShane 2012 (JMR). In observational debt-settlement data (about 6,000 debtors, *secondary*), closing small accounts first predicted eliminating the debt.
   - **Background:** the delay-discounting meta-analyses already in the register give a rationale for near-term wins in ADHD.
   - No ADHD-specific study exists.

   **Action:** keep consequence-first ranking as the default, but allow a "quick win first" option when starting (not choosing) is the bottleneck. Soften "easy isn't important" to "easy isn't automatically important".
4. **G12 (no streaks) is a defensible value choice, but the evidence cuts both ways.**
   - Silverman & Barasch 2023 (J Consumer Research, 7 studies, GP): showing an intact streak increases later engagement, and showing a broken one reduces it. The effect is stronger when people blame themselves for the break and weaker when the streak can be "repaired".
   - Mazeas et al. 2022 (meta-analysis of 16 RCTs, n = 2,407, physical activity, GP): gamification g = 0.42. The long-term effect is small: g = 0.15 at about 14 weeks.
   - There are no ADHD streak studies.

   The repo's "recovery after misses / no starting over" matches the "repair" moderator. **Action:** label "no streaks" as a value judgement backed by the broken-streak harm. Optionally allow opt-in, non-punitive counts with no reset framing.
5. **M7 (if-then cue-action pairs) is supported, but the register's effect size is out of date and the ADHD evidence is from children.**
   - The newest general-population meta-analysis, Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2025 (642 tests), gives d = .27 to .66. That is smaller than the d = .65 from Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 that the register uses.
   - The children's meta-analysis, Breitwieser & Reinelt 2026 (registered report, 42 studies, n = 12,957), gives g = 0.31. Effects were stronger "in some analyses" for children with ADHD.
   - Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008 found improved response inhibition in children with ADHD.
   - **No RCT in adults with ADHD was found.**
   - Altgassen et al. 2014 (25 ADHD adults vs 25 controls) found a large deficit in time-based prospective memory but none in event-based. So if-then cues should anchor to events or real alarms, not to "remember at 3 pm".
6. **G8 / M8 (no praise by default) has no direct evidence either way in adults with ADHD.** Adjacent evidence favours *informational* positive feedback:
   - Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (128 studies): positive feedback raised free-choice intrinsic motivation, d = 0.33. Tangible, contingent rewards undermined it.
   - Luman et al. 2005 (narrative review, 22 studies, n = 1,181 children): reinforcement improves performance, somewhat more in ADHD.
   - Marx et al. 2013 (adults): reward normalised one deficit.
   - Hornstra et al. 2023 (single-case, n = 6 children): adding praise gave no clear gain.

   The repo's "factual: what moved" style *is* informational feedback, so it fits the evidence. The risk is the "if wanted" gate turning feedback off. **Action:** make brief factual progress feedback on by default, keep effusive or controlling praise off, and label it a design default.

## 3. Notable gaps (no relevant literature found; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence)

- **Adults with ADHD:**
  - no RCT of implementation intentions or if-then planning (the only trials are in children);
  - no trial of Pomodoro or any fixed work/break schedule;
  - no study of praise or verbal encouragement, versus neutral feedback, on task follow-through;
  - no study of streaks or gamification on adherence.
- No peer-reviewed test of a "2-minute" or other micro-step starting rule in any population.
- No controlled trial of body doubling, human or AI. No study of an *AI* asking about suicide, or of chatbot crisis signposting, against outcomes.
- No ADHD-specific study of task ordering (quick wins vs high-consequence first).
- Time perception: the newest meta-analyses are lifespan or child-dominated. I did not find an adult-only meta-analysis after 2023, and the adult subgroup effect in Metcalfe 2024 needs the full text.
- Read-depth limit: everything above is abstract-level or secondary. The full texts of Metcalfe 2024, Marx 2022, Polihronis 2022 and Breitwieser 2026 should be read before the register is updated.
